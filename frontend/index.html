Questions

Frequently Asked

We use NLTK's VADER (Valence Aware Dictionary for sEntiment Reasoning) lexicon. Every English word is mapped to a polarity score — for example, "amazing" → +1.9, "terrible" → −2.5. VADER then computes a compound score between −1.0 and +1.0, which determines the overall sentiment classification: Positive (≥0.05), Negative (≤−0.05), or Neutral.

Unlike extractive summarizers that simply copy sentences from the source, our BART-Large-CNN model physically reads your text and generates entirely new sentences that distill the core meaning. It's the difference between highlighting existing text vs. writing a new paragraph from understanding.

Our spell corrector uses a custom-built Levenshtein distance algorithm (dynamic programming) to compute the minimum edit distance between your word and every candidate in a 230k+ English dictionary. It finds the closest real word by measuring the fewest insertions, deletions, and substitutions needed — no statistical shortcuts.

Perplexity measures how "surprised" the language model is by your text. Think of it as a fluency meter: a low score means your text flows naturally with predictable word sequences, while a high score indicates unusual grammar, rare vocabulary, or unexpected word combinations. It's computed as 2^(−average log₂ probability).
